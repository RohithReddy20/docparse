# Deep research request: beating LlamaParse on ParseBench charts and visual grounding with an open, CPU-heavy parser

## Who we are and what we have

We are building an open-source PDF parser evaluated on **ParseBench** (github.com/run-llama/ParseBench, paper arXiv:2604.08538, dataset huggingface.co/datasets/llamaindex/ParseBench — 2,078 PDF files, 1,211 docs, five dimensions: Tables, Charts, Content faithfulness, Semantic formatting, Visual grounding; scoring is deterministic and rule-based; the evaluator source is public in the repo under `src/parse_bench/evaluation/`).

Current full-set scores (overall = unweighted mean of the five):

| | table | chart | content | formatting | **visual grounding (layout)** | overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LightOnOCR-3-0.8B alone (VLM, grounding mode) | 83.5 | 61.0 | 88.9 | 64.7 | 66.0 | 72.8 |
| **ours** (text layer + VLM + PP-DocLayout-V3 fusion) | 83.5 | **70.4** | 89.0 | 70.6 | **73.8** | 77.5 |
| LlamaParse Cost Effective (0.375¢/page) | 84.2 | 77.9 | 89.9 | 67.3 | 83.8 | 80.6 |
| LlamaParse Agentic (1.25¢/page) | 88.9 | 88.7 | 91.8 | 81.4 | 84.3 | 87.0 |

Our pipeline (all open, Apache/MIT): PyMuPDF text layer (spans with font/size/bold/italic, underline/strike/highlight from vector drawings, `get_drawings()` for bars/lines/legend swatches, `get_image_info()`), **LightOnOCR-3-0.8B** in "grounding" mode via vLLM (block order, labels, bboxes on a 0–1000 grid, tables/charts as HTML), **PP-DocLayout-V3** (RT-DETR, 25 PP-DocLayout classes, with reading order) run over every page. Reconciliation is CPU Python: VLM characters kept, text-layer styling transferred onto them by fuzzy alignment, blocks split on paragraph gaps / list bullets, page header/footer lifted into structured fields; charts: VLM table snapped to printed data labels, extra candidate tables from text labels and from vector bars/lines scaled by axis ticks, `<caption>` with chart title + legend strings; layout elements = detector boxes with text attributed from the layer, VLM blocks only where the detector has nothing. Compute budget: we have a free T4/L4 GPU for ~1 hour per full run and unlimited CPU; the finished parser must cost well under 0.4¢/page.

## What the evaluator actually does (from its source)

- **Visual grounding** (`evaluators/layoutdet.py`): GT = 14,203 elements on 500 pages in 6 scored classes: Text 9,223, Section 3,001 (= Title + Section-header), Picture 2,286, Page-header 531, Page-footer 415, Table 232. GT granularity is detector-level: every list item, caption, footnote, key-value line, TOC row, form field is its own Text element; ~45% of Pictures are icons/logos. GT `source_label`s are PP-DocLayout/Docling-Heron names, i.e. the GT boxes were seeded by that detector family and then human-verified. Localization: per-GT argmax over predictions with IoA(GT,Pred) ≥ 0.50 AND IoA(Pred,GT) ≥ 0.20 (so a merged box passes for each element it covers if it is ≤ 5× that element; a split box passes only if one fragment alone covers ≥ 50% of the element). Classification: exact class on the best-localized box. Attribution: token-F1 ≥ 0.80 against the best overlapping box, merge-aware. Reading order scored separately.
- **Charts** (`metrics/parse/rules_chart.py`): 4,864 annotated data points over 568 pages; a point passes if its value appears in an HTML table cell (numbers normalized, relative tolerance per rule, mostly 1–5%) AND every label (typically [series, category], sometimes + chart title) is a substring (`partial_ratio`) of a `<th>` in that cell's row-header or column-header chain, or appears as a heading / bold line / `<caption>` in the 5 markdown lines before the table. Orientation-agnostic. Flat combined headers ("Primary 2015") satisfy two labels.

## Our measured failure modes

**Layout (element pass rate 74.2% under detector-primary fusion):** of 14,203 GT elements, localization fails on 1,810 (1,254 where our box is much bigger than the element: lists, KPI/infographic labels, stacked headings, captions merged into a paragraph box; 479 where our box is smaller/offset, mostly Pictures: 413 picture localization fails), classification fails on 1,201 after localization passes (Section→Text 473, Text→Section 238, Picture→Page-header 83, Text→Page-footer 67, Page-header→Text 67), attribution fails on 767. Text-layer features (bold, size vs body, length) do NOT separate Section from Text: the dominant pattern (bold, larger, short) appears in 648 correct Sections and 75 Text elements wrongly promoted; a heuristic promoting such lines made classification worse. Letting the detector's `text` label demote VLM headings cost 3 points of formatting on other documents. PP-DocLayout-V3 has no list-item class.

**Charts (70.4%):** 89% of chart pages are vector PDFs; only 42% of GT values are printed as text labels. Failures: ~1,200 "value found but labels not associated" (series name missing: legend not read or wrong; multi-panel small multiples where each panel's title is a label; multi-level headers region × category; stacked/grouped bars), ~780 "value not found" (lines/areas without data labels, pies, values the VLM hallucinated). Our vector reconstruction alone passes 11% of all points standalone; the VLM's own chart tables are right on values ~60% of the time but weak on series headers.

## What we want from this research

Concrete, implementable recipes and evidence, not overviews. Prioritize things runnable on CPU or a few GPU-minutes per 2,000 pages, with permissive licenses (Apache/MIT/BSD; AGPL ideas only as inspiration).

1. **Layout detection that matches ParseBench's conventions.** Compare openly available detectors on DocLayNet/Canonical17-style classes with list-item, caption, footnote, section-header, title, picture: Docling `docling-layout-heron` (RT-DETRv2) and `heron-101`, `docling-layout-egret` variants, DocLayout-YOLO, PP-DocLayout-V3, Surya layout, LayoutLMv3/DiT-based, any 2025–2026 release. For each: license, mAP on DocLayNet by class (especially list-item, section-header, caption, picture), inference cost, and how its boxes/labels map to the six scored classes. Which gives the best Section-vs-Text decision and picture boxes for icons/logos? Is there published work on *post-processing* detector output to a fixed granularity (splitting a text box into list items by hanging indent / bullet glyphs from the text layer; merging fragments; caption association) with reported gains — Docling's layout post-processor, PaddleX layout_parsing, Marker, MinerU 2.5, "Parser-Oriented Structural Refinement" (arXiv:2604.02692)? Give the exact rules.
2. **Classification of Section vs Text when fonts don't decide it.** What features or models do published systems use (context: preceding gap, following body text, numbering, position in column, repeated styles across the page, detector confidence)? Any small open model (<100M params) that takes a page image crop + text and predicts heading vs body with reported accuracy on DocLayNet or similar? Would a lightweight learned reranker over [detector label, VLM label, text-layer features] trained on public DocLayNet (NOT on ParseBench) plausibly reach >90% on this decision?
3. **Chart series/category association from vector PDFs.** Best documented algorithms for: legend detection and swatch↔mark colour matching (fills, strokes, dashes, markers, hatch patterns), stacked vs grouped bar clustering, axis calibration (linear/log/date axes, dual axes), line-chart sampling at category ticks, pie slices from Bézier arcs, small-multiple panel detection and titling — with reported accuracy (ChartDetective, Mystique, ReVision, ChartSense, D3 deconstruction, CHART-Infographics competition winners, Reducto's blog). Which open implementations exist in Python?
4. **Chart-to-table models for the raster / hallucination cases.** Among open models that fit a T4 (PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 chart mode, PP-Chart2Table, OneChart, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B, Granite-Docling, ChartMoE, TinyChart, others 2025–2026): reported chart-to-table accuracy *out of distribution* (WB-ChartExtract, EpiCurveBench, PaperUnPlot, ChartQA-SE), license, and whether their output carries series names from the legend. Prompt formats that reliably yield "one column per series, one row per category, headers copied verbatim from the legend/axis". Evidence for self-consistency / ensembling of chart tables.
5. **How LlamaParse Cost Effective gets 83.8 visual grounding and 77.9 charts at 0.375¢/page**, and how Agentic gets 88.7 charts: any public detail (blogs, talks, docs, ParseBench appendix, run-llama repos: LiteParse, ParseBench providers, llama_parse) on their layout detector, figure classifier, chart model, verification loops, and the page-decoration (`<page_header>`) convention.
6. **Benchmarks to validate on without touching ParseBench's GT**: public chart-to-table and layout datasets with the same conventions (DocLayNet, DocLayNet-v1.2, D4LA, OmniDocBench layout subset, PaperUnPlot, ChartQA-SE), so improvements can be developed on them and only confirmed on ParseBench.

## Output format

For each of the six questions: (a) the 2–4 best-supported approaches with numbers, licenses and links; (b) a step-by-step recipe we can implement in Python (PyMuPDF + detector boxes + optional small model), including thresholds; (c) expected gain on our failure counts above and the main risk. Finish with a ranked list of the top 10 actions by expected ParseBench gain per engineering day. Cite primary sources (papers, repos, docs); flag anything that is vendor marketing.
